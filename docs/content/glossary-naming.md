# Katon Naming — the two-layer rule

## THE RULE

**Indonesian name first. English term in brackets, once, at first mention. No Chinese characters.**

> Kamu punya **Bunga Persik** (Peach Blossom).
> **Fondasi Pasanganmu** ditempati **Aspek Penantang** (Seven Killings).

**The bracket is a citation, not a definition.** The reader never has to decode it to understand the
sentence. It is there to prove the system is real and to be googleable.

**This is why renaming does NOT kill legitimacy.** Legitimacy comes from the term being *checkable*,
not from it being *unreadable*. A user who searches "Seven Killings BaZi" finds a 2000-year-old
tradition and Katon's credibility goes up. A user who reads "Kamu Tujuh Pembunuh" with no gloss
either bounces or gets frightened. You keep the citation and drop the comprehension tax.

The layer that dies is the **untranslated** layer — 七殺, 正官, 偏印 as surface prose. Those were never
legitimacy, they were friction.

---

## FLAG: "10 Dewa" is a problem

Do not use **Dewa**. 神 in 十神 means *agent* or *aspect*, not deity. Indonesian "Dewa" reads as a
Hindu god, and for a Muslim-majority female audience that is at best odd and at worst a reason to
close the tab. This is a market risk, not a translation nicety.

**DECIDED (Reyner): "Sepuluh Aspek" (Ten Gods).** Accurate to the classical meaning, religiously
neutral, and *Aspek* reads as analysis rather than theatre — which is the positioning. Bracket it as
`(Ten Gods)` so the citation still works.

> Di peta **Sepuluh Aspek** (Ten Gods), kamu adalah ...

## THE TEN ASPECTS — decided 07-30

Locked English display names already exist in KATON-engine-session-state.md. These are the Indonesian
layer.

**TAXONOMY (Reyner, 07-30): ASPEK = internal disposition. BINTANG = external marker.**
This resolves the prefix collision and is classically defensible — 十神 are relational aspects of the
self, 神煞 are stars that mark you from outside. Two systems, two prefixes, never confusable.

| Classical | English (locked) | Indonesian | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 比肩 | The Self-Reliant | **Aspek Setara** | Standing on your own. Peers, not hierarchy. |
| 劫財 | The Mover | **Aspek Pendorong** | Competitive drive. Moves fast, spends fast. |
| 食神 | The Creator | **Aspek Karya** | Gentle output. Makes things, enjoys making them. |
| 傷官 | The Dazzler | **Aspek Pijar** | Sharp expression. Brilliant, breaks rules. |
| 正財 | The Steward | **Aspek Pengelola** | Steady stewardship. Holds what matters. |
| 偏財 | The Trailblazer | **Aspek Peluang** | Opportunity, windfall, big swings. |
| 正官 | The Keeper | **Aspek Pengatur** | Rules, duty, legitimate authority. |
| 七殺 | The Fighter | **Aspek Penantang** | Pressure, challenge, unconventional power. |
| 正印 | The Learner | **Aspek Pelindung** | Support, learning, being cared for. |
| 偏印 | The Thinker | **Aspek Pemikir** | Unusual insight, sideways thinking. |

`Aspek Pijar` replaces `Sorot` — *sorot* is a spotlight pointed AT you (passive, being watched).
*Pijar* is incandescence coming FROM you, which is what 傷官 actually is.
`Aspek Pendorong` replaces `Dorong` — noun, not verb, per the noun rule below.

Collective name: **Sepuluh Aspek (Ten Gods)**. Not "Dewa" — see flag above. Not "Peran", which is
faintly theatrical; *Aspek* reads as analysis, which is the positioning.

**"Seven Killings" is the one that must not stay literal.** Translated straight it is *Tujuh Pembunuh* —
violent, alarming, and misleading. The classical meaning is *unconventional authority under pressure*,
which is the opposite of alarming. `Bintang Penantang (Seven Killings)` keeps the citation and drops
the threat.

Note the bad-translation trap: "Direct Officer" and "Indirect Wealth" are artefacts of English
translation, not meaningful terms even in English. Do not preserve them out of respect. Bracket them
because they are searchable, and name them properly in Indonesian.

## PALACES — proposal

Replaces clauses like *"Rumah tempat hubungan paling dekatmu dibaca, yaitu pilar hari"*.

| Pillar | Katon name | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Year | **Pilar Akar** | Asal, keluarga, masa awal |
| Month | **Pilar Kerja** | Karier, lingkungan, orang di sekitar pekerjaan |
| Day | **Pilar Diri** | The self. Its branch = **Fondasi Pasangan** |
| Hour | **Pilar Arah** | Arah ke depan |

**DECIDED (Reyner, 07-30): Fondasi Pasangan.**

**Why NOT "Pilar Pasangan".** The day pillar is two characters. The **stem is the person** (Day
Master); only the **branch** is the spouse palace. "Pilar Pasangan" would make Pilar Diri and Pilar
Pasangan the same pillar — a coherence bug a reader can catch, and a factual error.

*Fondasi* is also classically exact: 日主坐下, the Day Master **sits on** that branch. It keeps the
mechanic teachable in one image and replaces a fourteen-word clause with two words.

## THE BADGES (symbolic stars)

**NOUN RULE (Reyner, 07-30): every badge is a noun or a noun phrase. Never noun+verb, never a bare
adjective.** A badge is a thing you carry, so it has to read like a thing.

| Classical | Katon | Bracket | Was |
|---|---|---|---|
| 桃花 | **Bunga Persik** | (Peach Blossom) | unchanged |
| 天乙貴人 | **Bintang Penolong** | (Nobleman) | unchanged |
| 驛馬 | **Bintang Perantau** | (Sky Horse) | ~~Bintang Jalan~~ |
| 空亡 | **Tanda Kekosongan** | (Void) | ~~Tanda Kosong~~ |
| 羊刃 | **Mata Pisau** | (Yang Blade) | unchanged |
| 文昌 | **Bintang Cendekia** | (Academic Star) | ~~Bintang Belajar~~ |
| 孤辰 | **Penyendiri** | (Solitary) | ~~Tanda Sendiri~~ |

**On 驛馬.** It is the post-horse: movement, relocation, careers that travel, restlessness that
resolves by going somewhere. *Kuda Langit* translates the English but carries none of the meaning.
**Bintang Perantau** lands it — *merantau* is a concept every Indonesian already owns, and it means
exactly this. That is a localisation win no foreign competitor can copy.

`Bintang Cendekia` over `Belajar` — noun, and more dignified.
`Penyendiri` needs no prefix; the word is already a person.

## WRITING THE label_meaning — the rule that fixes the "hard to understand" problem

**Describe an experience the reader has already had. Do not define a concept.**

> ✗ "menandai sesuatu yang nyata ada di petamu, tetapi sulit digenggam sebagai milik sendiri"
> ✓ "orang lain melihat kamu berhasil di bidang ini, dan kamu sendiri sering merasa belum pantas menyandangnya"

If a sentence could appear in a dictionary, rewrite it until it could only appear in a letter to this
person. **Abstraction is the failure mode**, and it was the cause of both "hard to understand" notes
on run 3 — the input was abstract, so the output was.

Do NOT name clinical conditions. "Impostor syndrome" is a psychological claim Katon has no business
making. Describe the feeling; let the reader supply the label.

| Set | Count | Each |
|---|---|---|
| Ten Roles | 10 | name + label_meaning + gift/cost seed |
| Symbolic stars | ~8 | name + label_meaning + gift/cost seed |
| Animals (branches) | 12 | name + one-line character |
| Elements | 5 | name + what it is, what feeds it, what drains it |
| Missing element | 5 | label_meaning + gift/cost |
| Branch relations | ~20 | combine / trine / clash / harm |
| Palaces | 4 | short name + what it covers |
| **Total** | **~64** | 2–3 sentences each |

More entries than the old 78 modules, far simpler each, and **none of it is chart-specific prose** —
it is written once and reused by everyone who carries that badge. Chart-specific text stays in
`gift`/`cost`/`actionable`, which the engine conditions on palace and context.

**The leverage: these badges are also the sharecard tags.** One glossary serves the card and the
reading. Two content tasks collapse into one.
